Why my lessons are different

Individuality is the point, not a side effect

Generic voices bore me.

A singer can be technically perfect and completely forgettable. That is not a compliment to the teaching, it is a failure of it. Technique exists to give you access to your own voice, not to replace it with somebody else's.

So when we work together I am not trying to make you sound like the artist you admire. I am trying to find out what you sound like when nothing is in the way.

That usually includes the bits you think are wrong. A little roughness, an unusual texture, a break you have been told to hide. Often that is the most interesting thing about your voice, and the thing that makes people listen.

What I will not do

  • Run you through the same exercise routine I give everyone else
  • Train the character out of your voice
  • Teach you to imitate somebody more famous
  • Tell you it was great when it was not

The note is usually not the problem

Watch a singer approach the line they are dreading and you will see it before you hear it. The face changes. The body braces. They start pushing before they reach the note.

The note was never the issue. The anticipation of the note was.

That is what I listen and look for. Not just the sound, but the flinch before it. Then we make one small change: the mouth shape, the vowel, the pressure, where you place it, what you are thinking about, sometimes just what you are picturing while you sing it.

Small adjustment. Different voice.

What actually happens in your hour

No two lessons are identical, because no two singers are. This is the shape most of them take.

First 5 minutes

How are you, how is your voice, what has happened since last time, what are we working on. If I hear tiredness or strain, I change the plan.

Next 10

Sounds, sirens, patterns, chosen for you and for today. I am listening for pressure, break points, tension, whether you are pushing.

Next 10

We isolate the one thing that matters most. I explain it. If that explanation does not land, I explain it a different way. Then another. I keep changing the language until it lands.

Next 20

Into a real song. Your song. I stop at the exact word. I tell you what I heard. I show you the alternative. You do it. We put it back in.

Next 10

A longer section, to see whether the change survives in context.

Last 5

What we found, what is next, what to practise, where to look in the recording.

Professional work

The work behind the teaching

This is what a £85 hour is actually built on. Alongside the people who have never had a lesson in their life.

English pronunciation for international artists

A large part of my professional work is English diction for artists whose first language is not English. Vowels, consonants, diphthongs, stress and fluency, worked word by word until the line sounds natural rather than learned. It is a specialist skill, there are very few people who do it at this level, and it is one of the main reasons producers call me.

Film and recording

I work with a film director and his artists on the vocals for a film's lead song. That is pronunciation, phrasing and delivery, getting a specific performance to land in the studio, to a deadline, with other people in the room and opinions flying.

Stadium and tour preparation

I prepare artists for tours and large venue performances. That work is stamina, consistency and making sure a voice survives night after night, plus the songs themselves. When thousands of people have paid to be there, the voice cannot be a variable.

International artists

I work with established performers and recording artists with international careers, particularly across Spain and India, as well as actors and screen performers who are required to sing. Some have been with me for many years.

More about film, recording and English diction work

I do not name clients on this website. Some work is confidential, and the rest is not mine to advertise. If you are booking me for a project and need references, ask me directly.

How I teach

Getting into the singer's mind so they can get out of their mind.

Every voice is different, so every route is different. I work out whether what is blocking you is technical, psychological, stylistic or linguistic, because treating a confidence problem with a breathing exercise wastes your money.

Then the smallest change that works. Mouth shape, vowel, pressure, placement, what you are picturing, sometimes just permission to stop trying so hard.

I will tell you the truth about what I hear, directly, without humiliating you. That is the same whether it is your first lesson or a session for a film.

Three transformations

What unlocking a voice actually looks like

Three notes to three and a half octaves

He arrived with three notes. Not three octaves, three notes, and that was his entire vocal range. I told him honestly that he might be happier playing another instrument.

He asked me to teach him anyway. So we did it properly, intensively, almost a crash course, and we unlocked the whole thing. He ended up with over three and a half octaves and went on to become a recording artist who is now well known in India.

It is the biggest job I have ever done, and it changed the course of my career as much as his.

An average singer, and my first ever student

A regular at the restaurant I managed kept asking me to teach her husband. I kept saying no. Eventually I agreed to six lessons just to end the conversation.

He was an average singer when he walked in. He booked more lessons, then more, and he became genuinely outstanding. He told everyone he knew, a London management company found me because of it, and that is the only reason I do this for a living.

The moment the penny drops

A drag performer who was already good, already building a name, and singing live rather than miming. Resonance was the thing that would not land. It took a while, as it often does.

Then one lesson he looked up with a huge grin and said, "I get you. I completely get everything you're talking about." I can always see that moment arrive.

His voice opened after that. He went from a very good singer to a genuinely remarkable one, and he has not stopped since.

Almost every singer I meet has more in them than they realise. My job is getting them to hear what I can already hear.

Common questions

I have never had a singing lesson. Is that a problem?

No. Complete beginners are the most common way people arrive here, and the approach is the same one I use with professionals. Nothing is assumed and nothing is rushed.

What if I sing badly in the consultation?

Then you sing badly, and we both learn something useful. One nervous attempt does not define your voice, and I will say so before you start.

Are online singing lessons as good as in person?

For one-to-one coaching, yes. I can hear pitch, tone, breath, strain and register changes clearly, and I can see tension and anticipation on your face. Lessons are live on Microsoft Teams, in real time, never pre-recorded.
