What I actually do

I am not really in the business of teaching people to sing. I am in the business of finding out what is in the way and removing it.

Almost every singer who comes to me can already do more than they think. Something is sitting between them and it. Sometimes it is technical, a mouth shape, a vowel, too much pressure in the wrong place. Sometimes it is psychological, the fear that arrives a full bar before the difficult note. Sometimes it is stylistic, or linguistic, or simply that nobody has ever explained the thing in a way their brain could use.

My job in the first ten minutes is to work out which of those it is. Get that wrong and you can spend a year doing breathing exercises for a confidence problem. That happens to people constantly, and it is why they arrive at my door convinced they have no talent.

How I teach

Getting into the singer's mind so they can get out of their mind.

Once I know what is actually blocking you, I look for the smallest change that shifts it. Not a programme, not a six week course. One adjustment. The mouth shape, the vowel, the pressure, where you place the sound, what you are picturing, sometimes just permission to stop trying so hard.

Then I explain it. If that explanation does not land, that is my problem to fix, not proof that you are slow. So I explain it another way, and another. Some people need the physical sensation, some need the picture, some need to know exactly what the vocal folds are doing, some need a ridiculous comparison about drinking through a narrow straw. I will keep going until one of them works, because one of them always does.

And then it goes straight back into your song. Not a scale. The actual thing you came here wanting to sing. Exercises are useful, but an exercise that has drifted away from why you walked in is just homework.

What I believe about voices

Every singer has more in them than they realise. I see it well before they do. Most of my job is getting someone to hear what I can already hear.

Technique should not flatten you. Generic voices bore me. A singer can be word perfect, pitch perfect and utterly forgettable, and that is a failure of the teaching, not a triumph of it. The roughness you have been told to hide is often the most interesting thing about your voice.

The note is rarely the problem. Watch someone approach the line they dread and you will see it before you hear it. The face changes. The body braces. They start pushing before they arrive. Fix the anticipation and the note tends to look after itself.

You have to be allowed to be bad. Nobody learns while they are performing for approval. In my lessons you can make a mess of it, and nothing happens.

You get the truth. I will tell you exactly what I heard. Directly, specifically, and without humiliating you. That is the same whether it is your first lesson or a session for a film. Agreeing with someone to keep them comfortable is not coaching, it is customer service.

The professional side

I taught in person in London for more than fifteen years before moving my teaching entirely online, and I now work from a mobile studio, which means I can teach from wherever I happen to be in the world.

Alongside the people who have never had a lesson in their life, my work includes:

English pronunciation for international artists

English diction for performers whose first language is not English. Vowels, consonants, stress and fluency, worked word by word until a line sounds natural rather than learned. Very few coaches do this at the level it is needed for recording, and it is one of the main reasons producers call me.

Film and recording sessions

Working with a director and his artists on the vocals for a film's lead song. Pronunciation, phrasing, delivery, and getting a performance to land in the studio to a deadline.

Stadium and tour preparation

Preparing artists for tours and large venue performances. Stamina, consistency, and making sure a voice survives night after night when thousands of people have paid to be there.

Established international artists

Recording artists and performers with international careers, particularly across Spain and India, plus actors and screen performers required to sing. Some have worked with me for many years.

I do not name clients here. Some of the work is confidential and the rest is not mine to advertise. If you are booking me for a project and need references, ask me directly.

Why I teach the way I do

I have ADHD. I know what it is like to be told something genuinely useful and lose it before you get home, and to be called lazy when you were working harder than anyone in the room.

That is why nothing in my teaching depends on your memory. After every lesson you get a link to your own folder containing the recording, a written summary of what we worked on, and a clear path telling you exactly what to practise next. It stays there for you.

It is also why I never blame a student for not understanding an explanation, and why I will happily find the sixth way to say something. More on how I teach neurodivergent singers
