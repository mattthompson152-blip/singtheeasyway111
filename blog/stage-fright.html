Performance context

Singing with Performance Nerves

Updated 2 November 2026

By Matt Thompson

Nerves can change how a familiar song feels. This article concerns musical coaching observations, not mental-health treatment or a guarantee that anxiety will disappear.

Find the point where the task changes

Consider the exact point where attention shifts: waiting to begin, seeing the audience or approaching a demanding phrase. Different moments call for different practical preparation.

Coaching is not anxiety treatment

You do not need to feel calm before making a musical choice. If anxiety is severe or affects life beyond singing, an appropriately qualified healthcare professional can offer support outside vocal coaching.

Prepare in steps you choose

Practise the transition into the song as well as the song itself. Build familiarity in small steps and do not force exposure or push through distress.

Include the moments around the song

Make a simple sequence of the performance task: waiting, being introduced, finding your starting point, singing, and finishing. Which moment do you want to prepare? You might rehearse the opening breath or the first line in a familiar setting, but you can choose a different focus or stop at any point.

Afterward, describe what happened without treating one performance as a measure of your ability or a test of whether nerves are gone. If distress is severe, persistent, or affects life beyond singing, seek help from an appropriately qualified healthcare professional. Vocal coaching can stay with musical preparation and cannot provide mental-health treatment.
