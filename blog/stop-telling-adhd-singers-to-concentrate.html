The Stupidest Thing You Can Tell a Singer With ADHD

First published 18 November 2026

By Matt Thompson

“Concentrate harder” sounds like advice. Usually, it tells the singer nothing.

I have ADHD and have coached singers for 25 years. When attention disappears, I do not assume the singer lacks discipline. I ask whether the task deserves their attention.

Repetitive sounds with no audible target can become empty labour. A problem inside a song is different: Which vowel changes the phrase? Where does the lyric lose meaning? What can the singer hear improving now?

Research describes ADHD attention and motivation as variable and sensitive to context. Music education studies also indicate that ADHD-related behaviours can change when learners are actively engaged in music. That does not mean every ADHD singer learns identically or that interest removes every difficulty.

My responsibility is not to demand focus as though it were obedience. It is to make the work precise enough to engage curiosity, with enough structure to turn that curiosity into repeatable professional choices.
