When Vocal Exercises Do Not Solve the Problem in Your Song

Published 15 November 2026 · Updated 16 November 2026

By Matt Thompson

Vocal exercises can be useful because they isolate sounds and reduce complexity. But success in an exercise does not always transfer to the song.

A real lyric introduces changing vowels, consonants, rhythm, pitch and intensity. If a phrase feels easy on one sound but the difficulty returns with the words, the exercise may have removed what needs attention.

Bring the song back into the work. Isolate the difficult line, explore it at a manageable pitch, identify which sound changes the result, then restore the original lyric, rhythm and key.

Do not ask only, “Is this a good exercise?” Ask why you are doing it, what should change and whether that change remains in the song.

The objective is not to become good at exercises. It is to develop choices that remain available in contemporary music.
