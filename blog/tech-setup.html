Preparing for Online Singing Lessons: Essential Tech Setup Guide

You need far less equipment than you think. A device with a camera and a quiet room will do.

I have taught international recording artists over a laptop. I would rather you spent nothing and booked a lesson than spent three hundred pounds on an interface and put the lesson off for another month.

If you want to improve one thing, improve the room, not the microphone. Soft furnishings beat expensive gear.

Online singing lessons have revolutionised how we learn to sing. Whether you're taking your first lesson or you're a seasoned vocalist, having the right tech setup can make the difference between a frustrating experience and a transformative one. Here's everything you need to know to prepare for your online singing lessons.

Your Microphone Matters More Than You Think

For online singing lessons, your microphone is your voice's ambassador. While built-in laptop microphones work in a pinch, they compress your vocal range and miss subtle tonal qualities your teacher needs to hear. A USB condenser microphone (£50-£150) captures the full spectrum of your voice, allowing your coach to identify register transitions, breath support issues, and resonance placement accurately.

Pro tip: Position your microphone at nose height, approximately 6-8 inches away, angled slightly downward. This captures your voice without excessive breathing noise or plosives.

Do not let setup become the reason you do not start. I have taught international recording artists over a laptop microphone. The gear is the least interesting part of this and it is very often where people hide when they are nervous about booking.

Internet Speed: The Silent Lesson Killer

Nothing disrupts online singing lessons like lag or dropped audio. You need a minimum of 5 Mbps upload speed for clear audio transmission. Video requires 10 Mbps or higher. Before your lesson, close unnecessary applications and ask household members to pause downloads or streaming.

Wired ethernet connections outperform Wi-Fi every time. If you must use Wi-Fi, position yourself close to your router and reduce interference from other devices.

Room Acoustics on a Budget

Your room affects how your teacher hears you. Hard, empty rooms create echo that masks vocal clarity. Soft furnishings absorb excess sound. Position yourself in a carpeted room if possible, or hang a thick duvet behind your singing space. Avoid corners where bass frequencies build up unnaturally.

One thing genuinely worth doing. Use headphones or earbuds. It stops my voice leaking back into your microphone and means I hear you clearly rather than a blend of both of us. That is the single biggest improvement most people can make, and it costs nothing.

Lighting and Camera Position

Your teacher observes posture, facial tension, and mouth shape—crucial elements of vocal technique. Position a light source behind your camera, facing you. Avoid backlighting from windows behind you. Your camera should capture you from the waist up, allowing your teacher to see breathing mechanics and posture.

Backup Plans Save Lessons

Technology fails. Have a backup device ready, keep your teacher's contact details handy, and test your setup 15 minutes before each lesson. Record your lessons (with permission) so you can review feedback later.

The Platform Makes a Difference

Zoom, Skype, and Google Meet each handle audio differently. Zoom's "Original Sound" mode (found in audio settings) disables noise suppression that often mistakes singing for background noise. Enable this for the most accurate vocal transmission during your online singing lessons.

Ready to Start?

With proper preparation, online singing lessons offer convenience without compromising quality. The technology exists to support excellent vocal training—you simply need to set it up correctly.
