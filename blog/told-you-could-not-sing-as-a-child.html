Being Told You Could Not Sing As A Child

First published 15 September 2026 · Updated 2 November 2026

By Matt Thompson

A remembered criticism can remain vivid. It can explain why singing feels loaded for someone, but it cannot establish a lasting verdict about their voice.

A past comment is not a voice assessment

Separate the event from the conclusion attached to it. A teacher’s comment, choir audition or being asked to mime was one experience in one context.

Keep the work in the present

You do not have to revisit or disclose a painful memory in a lesson. Coaching can stay with present-day musical choices and the level of exposure you choose.

Choose what you want to share

Choose a familiar, low-pressure song and notice what feels comfortable today. If the process brings distress beyond what you want to manage in coaching, pause and seek suitable support.

Use a present-day question, not an old verdict

Pick one current musical task you would like to understand, such as starting a phrase, keeping words clear or choosing a comfortable song. Describe what you hear now before relating it to something said years ago. A memory may matter to you, but it does not tell a coach what your present voice can do.

You can keep the conversation entirely in the present. You are not required to retell a painful event, prove that the comment was wrong, or take on a more exposed song. If the memory brings distress you do not want to manage in a coaching session, pause and seek support from an appropriately qualified professional.

Focus on a present-day question

Bring a present-day song or question to a paid Consultation if you want an individual coaching perspective. It is £25 for 30 minutes, not a free assessment.
