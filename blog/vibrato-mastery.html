Vibrato as a Musical Choice

Updated 2 November 2026

By Matt Thompson

Vibrato is not a requirement for every singer or every musical phrase. Whether it belongs depends on the style, the note and the sound you want.

Decide whether the song calls for vibrato

Listen to a sustained note at comfortable pitch. Notice whether the sound remains steady, changes naturally or becomes less predictable under effort; one recording cannot explain why.

Do not force a particular sound

Trying to manufacture a wide or fast vibrato can create unnecessary tension. Vibrato should not be promised as a technique every voice can produce in the same way.

Listen at a comfortable intensity

Compare a comfortable sustained note with the same note in a song. Do not push volume or sustain beyond comfort. For persistent voice change or pain, stop and seek healthcare advice.

Ask whether the sound belongs in the phrase

Listen to a sustained note in the style you are singing and decide whether a steady sound or a changing pitch quality better suits the musical moment. Compare with a recording only as a stylistic reference; singers and styles differ, and a recording cannot tell you what your voice should do.

Do not shake, push or hold a note to manufacture an effect. If a sound appears naturally, you can still choose whether it serves the lyric and phrase. If it feels effortful, unfamiliar or uncomfortable, return to a comfortable sound rather than trying to force a particular width or speed. A coach can discuss the musical choice, not promise that every singer will produce an identical effect.
