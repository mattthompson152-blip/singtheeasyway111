Perfectionism and Singing: When Monitoring Interrupts Music

First published 14 September 2026 · Updated 2 November 2026

By Matt Thompson

Wanting to sing well is not the problem. Difficulty begins when judging every sound in real time leaves no attention for the next word or musical idea.

Notice when self-monitoring begins

Notice where the commentary appears: before a high note, after a missed pitch, or while listening back. Its timing can suggest which part of the task draws attention away.

Give the phrase one clear focus

Accuracy and expression are not opposites, but they compete when you evaluate every detail at once. A recording can help you review later; it need not become a scorecard.

Review without grading yourself

Sing one passage with a single intention, such as clear words or a steady phrase. Review only that point, then choose another focus. Stop if practice becomes distressing or physically uncomfortable.

Give listening and singing separate jobs

Trying to perform a phrase while judging every note can make the task feel crowded. Decide on one intention before you sing; save detailed listening for afterward. When you replay a recording, listen once for that intention and write down one observation before deciding whether another listen would answer a different question.

This does not mean lowering every standard or ignoring a mistake. It means choosing when to evaluate it. If you hear a specific point to revisit, isolate that short moment and return it to the full phrase rather than replaying the entire song for a global verdict. If repeated review becomes distressing, pause and choose another way to work.
