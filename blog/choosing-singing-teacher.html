Choosing a Singing Coach: Questions Worth Asking

Updated 2 November 2026

By Matt Thompson

Choosing a coach is not a contest to find the biggest list of claims. You need to understand how the person will listen to your goal, explain their choices and respond when an approach does not help.

Ask how the coach listens

Ask how they assess a song-specific difficulty, how they decide what to work on first, and how an exercise connects back to music. Their answers should be clear enough for you to question.

Look for explanation and adaptation

A credential or teaching style alone cannot guarantee a good fit. Be cautious of guaranteed outcomes, pressure to buy a package, or advice to continue through pain.

Keep your choice in your hands

State one musical problem and ask how they would begin exploring it. You should understand the proposed next step and be free to decide whether it suits you.

Notice how the coach responds to uncertainty

A useful conversation leaves room for “I do not know yet”. Ask what the coach would listen for next, how you would decide whether a proposed adjustment helped, and what they would do if it did not. The answer should connect to your song and goal, rather than asking you to accept an unexplained exercise or a guaranteed result.

You can also ask how the work is delivered, what the session costs and where coaching stops. Pain, persistent voice changes and health concerns are not occasions for a coach to diagnose or promise protection. You do not have to buy a package or share personal information to assess whether the explanation is clear and the proposed next step feels appropriate.
